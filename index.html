AI translation, inside the CAT tool you already use.

Feed an AI one sentence at a time and you get good sentences and a poor translation. Supervertaler gives it what a translator would have: the rest of the document, your confirmed segments, your terminology, what you know about the client – even the list numbering and figures the grid never shows.

It reads like a translation by someone who has read the whole document – because the AI has.

Context layers

Every request assembles a fresh snapshot of your work – ten layers of it – and hands the whole thing to the AI. Some layers come from the CAT tool, some from what you have recorded about the client, and two from parts of the document no CAT tool normally shows you at all: the list numbering Word generates rather than stores, and the figures.

The ten layers, for Trados  ·  and for memoQ

Tested on a real patent

A published Dutch patent, translated in memoQ by a model you can run on your own computer and by Claude Opus 5.5, then compared paragraph by paragraph with its official English. Six house rules added to SuperMemory, one line each, were applied on the very next run, and Claude flagged a drafting error in the Dutch claims.
